Selah: Study Grounds

Your study time, as a place you can walk into.

Selah is a visual record of the hours you actually put in. Not a streak you can break, not a chart, not a number that shames you — a home base that gets a little more built every time you sit down. The focus timer lives inside it, so studying and building are the same button.

Downloaded it? Read this before you open it

⚠️ Read this firstOpening it the first time

Makullveny is not code-signed yet — signing certificates cost more than this project makes, which is nothing. Both systems will stop the first launch. Here is the way past, once.

Windows
  1. Run the installer. Windows shows a blue “Windows protected your PC” box.
  2. Click More info — the small link under the message.
  3. Click Run anyway.
Two clicks, once. It will not ask again.
macOS
  1. Drag Makullveny into Applications, then try to open it once. macOS refuses.
  2. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll down.
  3. Beside “Makullveny was blocked”, click Open Anyway, then Open.
Or in Terminal, once: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Makullveny.app
Windows 10 or newer · macOS 11 or newer · a recent Linux desktop.

Privacy

Your work stays yours.

Makullveny is local-first by design. This page is a public overview of the project; the app's source is private, and so is everything you write in it.

Notes stay local

Books, pages, drawings and study content are stored on your own computer unless you choose to export or move them.

Private work stays private

This public page is only a project overview. It does not include the private app source or anyone's study data.

Accounts stay optional

You can write, draw, revise and convert without ever making one. An account only buys you the optional extras — cloud backup and sharing — and the app is complete without it.

And keep checking Canvas, Blackboard, or whatever your school actually uses.

Makullveny reads what your calendar feed gives it at the moment you import. It cannot see an assignment your school posted afterwards, one that was changed, one hidden behind a login, or one an instructor only said out loud in class. Treat this app as the place you work, and your school’s own site as the place you check — and check it often. If the two ever disagree, your school is right.
