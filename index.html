Your study time, as a place you can walk into.

Selah is a visual record of the hours you actually put in. Not a streak you can break, not a chart, not a number that shames you — a home base that gets a little more built every time you sit down. The focus timer lives inside it, so studying and building are the same button.

Download

Getting Makullveny onto your computer.

It is free, and it stays free. There are a few short steps and one confusing moment in the middle — here is all of it, in order, with a picture of every screen you will meet.

Why your computer is about to warn you

Windows and Apple each sell a certificate that vouches for who wrote an app — around $100 a year for one and a few hundred for the other. Makullveny is free and earns nothing, so it does not have them yet. That is the whole reason for the warning: not that something is wrong with the app, only that nobody has paid to vouch for it. Both systems will let you say “I know — open it”, and both of them hide that button. Below is exactly where.

💻 Makullveny is desktop software — it does not run on a phone or a tablet. Open this page on a computer when you are ready, and the steps below are waiting.

1

Get the installer

One file, straight from the releases page. Nothing runs on its own — it waits in your Downloads folder until you open it.

Chrome, Edge and Firefox all tuck a finished download into a small panel near the toolbar. Opening it from there saves the trip to the folder.

3

Windows stops you once. This is the part to read.

A coloured box appears saying Windows protected your PC. Nothing is wrong with the file — the box is about who signed it, not what is in it.

The catch is that the button you need is not on the box when it arrives. “Run anyway” only appears after you press “More info” — and that one small link is where nearly everybody gives up.

The box borrows your Windows accent colour, so yours may be blue, green or purple rather than this. The words are the same.

✓

The installer runs, Makullveny opens, and neither Windows nor SmartScreen asks again — not for this version, and not for the updates after it.

One last thing worth thirty seconds: while Makullveny is open, right-click it on the taskbar at the bottom of your screen and choose Pin to taskbar. It stays there from now on, so opening it tomorrow is one click instead of a hunt through the Start menu.

About the project, and where a bug goes

Makullveny is made by two brothers, and it is being built in front of you.

It is a TDG project — The Disciples of God, the same two of us behind the other apps on the TDG site. The whole app is a work in progress: we are releasing early on purpose, because we would rather students and friends were using it now and telling us what breaks than wait on a version that never quite feels finished.

So expect bugs — anywhere in the app, not only in the newest parts. When you hit one, telling us is the fastest way it gets fixed, and it is genuinely the main way to support what we are doing: every report, and every person who actually uses the app, is what keeps it moving.

Report a bug See the rest of TDG →

Report a bug at any time. The button opens TDG’s feedback form with Makullveny already selected; you can send the same report from inside the app — Settings → Feedback → Send Feedback — and a reply comes back to you there.

1
Say what you were doing

The page you were on and the button you pressed is usually the whole bug.

2
Say what happened instead

Screenshots help. So does the exact wording of anything that appeared.

3
Send it from the app if you can

Reports from inside Makullveny carry the version number with them, and the answer lands back in the app.

And keep checking Canvas, Blackboard, or whatever your school actually uses.

Makullveny reads what your calendar feed gives it at the moment you import. It cannot see an assignment your school posted afterwards, one that was changed, one hidden behind a login, or one an instructor only said out loud in class. Treat this app as the place you work, and your school’s own site as the place you check — and check it often. If the two ever disagree, your school is right.

Privacy

Your work stays yours.

Makullveny is local-first by design. This page is a public overview of the project; the app's source is private, and so is everything you write in it.

Notes stay local

Books, pages, drawings and study content are stored on your own computer unless you choose to export or move them.

Private work stays private

This public page is only a project overview. It does not include the private app source or anyone's study data.

Accounts stay optional

You can write, draw, revise and convert without ever making one. An account only buys you the optional extras — cloud backup and sharing — and the app is complete without it.
